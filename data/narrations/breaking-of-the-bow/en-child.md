Long ago, King Janaka welcomed the wise sage Vishwamitra and two princes, Rama and Lakshmana, to his kingdom one bright morning. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] He bowed to the sage and asked what he could do for him. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] Vishwamitra said the two princes wished to see the great bow that Janaka kept, and asked him to show it to them. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]]

Janaka told them the story of the bow. Long ago it belonged to the god Shiva, who had once been angry with the gods and threatened them with it. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] The gods prayed to Shiva, and he was pleased and gave the bow to them, and it was passed down as a trust until it came to Janaka's family. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]]

Janaka also told them about his daughter, Sita. Once, while he was ploughing a field for a sacrifice, he found a girl who had risen from the earth, and she grew up as his own daughter. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] Janaka decided that only someone who could bend his great bow would be allowed to marry Sita. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] Many kings had come and tried, but not one of them could even lift the bow, so Janaka sent them all away. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] Angry at this, some kings attacked his city, Mithila, for a whole year, until Janaka prayed to the gods for help. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] The gods gave him a great army, and the attacking kings were driven away. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]] Now, Janaka said, he would let Rama try to string the bow, and if he could, Sita would become his wife. [[ramayana:bala:66:1]]

So Janaka ordered his ministers to bring out the bow. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] It was so heavy that it lay in an iron box on a huge cart with eight wheels, and five thousand strong men had to pull it. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] Janaka told everyone that not even gods or great spirits had ever been able to string this bow. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]]

Then Rama stepped forward and opened the case. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] He asked if he might touch the bow and try to bend it, and Janaka and Vishwamitra told him to go ahead. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] In front of a huge crowd, Rama picked up the bow easily, fixed the string, and pulled it back. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] With a sound like a thunderclap, the great bow snapped in two! [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] The noise was so loud that the ground shook, and everyone in the crowd fell over except for Janaka, Vishwamitra, Rama, and Lakshmana. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]]

When everyone had calmed down, Janaka joined his hands together and told Vishwamitra that he could hardly believe what he had just seen. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] He said his daughter Sita would now marry Rama, just as he had promised. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]] Janaka happily sent messengers riding to Ayodhya to bring King Dasaratha the wonderful news, and to invite him to come to Mithila. [[ramayana:bala:67:1]]
