Long, long ago, there was a shining golden mountain called Meru, so tall that ordinary people could not even imagine climbing it. [[mahabharata:adi:17:1]] The gods sat together on its highest peak, all of them wishing for amrita, the nectar that could make them live forever. [[mahabharata:adi:17:1]] Narayana told them what to do: the gods and the Asuras should churn the great Ocean together, and out of it would come the nectar, and other precious things too. [[mahabharata:adi:17:1]]

To churn the Ocean, they needed a giant stick, so they chose a huge mountain called Mandara. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]] The gods could not pull it up themselves, so Vishnu and Brahman sent the great snake Ananta, who tore the whole mountain up, trees, animals and all, and carried it to the sea. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]] The Ocean agreed to be churned if it got a share of the nectar too, and the king of the tortoises offered his own back so the mountain would not sink, Indra setting it down on top of him. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]]

For a rope, they wound a giant snake named Vasuki around the mountain. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]] The Asuras held his head and the gods held his tail, and together they pulled back and forth, turning the mountain like a great churning stick. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]] Vasuki puffed out smoke and fire from the strain, which turned into rain clouds that cooled the tired gods, while flowers fell from the trees on the mountain. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]] The Ocean roared, trees crashed into the water, and fires broke out until Indra put them out with rain. [[mahabharata:adi:18:1]]

After churning a long time, sticky sap from the trees mixed into the water, and the water slowly turned thick like butter, but the nectar still had not come. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]] The gods grew tired and told Brahman they had no strength left, so Narayana gave them fresh strength to keep churning. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]]

Wonderful things rose out of the Ocean one by one: first the gentle Moon, then the goddess Lakshmi dressed all in white, then a beautiful white horse, and a glowing jewel called Kaustubha. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]] Then the healer Dhanwantari rose up, carrying a white pot of nectar, and the Asuras shouted that it should belong to them. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]] A huge white elephant named Airavata came up too, and Indra took him. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]] Then a terrible poison called Kalakuta rose from the water, and its smoke frightened everyone, so the god Shiva swallowed it to keep the world safe; it stayed in his throat forever after, which is why he is called Nilakantha, the blue-throated one. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]] The Asuras got ready to fight the gods for the nectar, but Narayana turned into a beautiful woman with his magic and charmed the Asuras until they placed the pot of nectar into her hands. [[mahabharata:adi:18:2]]

The Asuras attacked the gods, but Narayana, still in disguise and helped by Nara, carried the nectar away, and the gods began to drink it happily. [[mahabharata:adi:19:1]] One Asura named Rahu snuck in disguised as a god to drink too, but the sun and moon, Surya and Soma, spotted him and told everyone. [[mahabharata:adi:19:1]] Just as the nectar touched Rahu's throat, Narayana cut off his head with his spinning discus, but because he had already tasted the nectar, his head stayed alive and flew up into the sky, crying out. [[mahabharata:adi:19:1]] That is why people say Rahu's head still chases the sun and moon, swallowing them during an eclipse. [[mahabharata:adi:19:1]]

A great battle followed, and Narayana's spinning discus, Sudarsana, struck down Asuras by the thousand. [[mahabharata:adi:19:1]] The Asuras hurled mountains down from the sky, but Nara broke them into dust with his golden arrows, until the Asuras fled, some into the earth and others into the sea. [[mahabharata:adi:19:2]] The gods put Mandara back where it belonged, and carried the nectar home to the heavens, giving the pot to Narayana to keep safe. [[mahabharata:adi:19:2]]
