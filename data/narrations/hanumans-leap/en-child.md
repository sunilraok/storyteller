Ravana had carried Sita far away across the sea, and Hanuman set out to find her. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] He stood on Mount Mahendra, a mountain full of colorful metals and wonderful beings, and got ready for the hardest task of his life: crossing the great ocean. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]

He folded his hands and bowed to the Sun, to Indra, to the Wind who was his father, and to Brahma, asking for their blessing on his journey. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Then, facing south toward Lanka, he began to grow bigger and bigger, the way the sea grows bigger at the full moon. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] He pressed down on the mountain with his arms and legs to leap, and the whole mountain shook. Flowers fell from every tree, water gushed out like from an elephant, and bright veins of gold and silver showed in the rock. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Snakes hissed and animals cried out, and people living nearby were frightened and flew into the sky, until wise sages understood: this was Hanuman, son of the Wind, setting out to help Rama. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]

Hanuman roared, gathered all his strength, and called out to the other monkeys, "I will fly to Lanka as fast as an arrow. If I find Sita, I will bring back good news. If I cannot find her, I will not stop trying." [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Then he leapt into the sky. The trees on the mountain were pulled up by the force of his jump and followed him a little way, like friends saying goodbye, before falling into the sea. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] He flew on and on, and the Sun did not burn him, and the Wind helped him along, because he was doing this for Rama. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]

The Ocean remembered that Rama's own ancestor, King Sagara, had once been kind to it, so it wanted to help Hanuman rest. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] It asked a golden mountain named Mainaka, hidden under the water, to rise up and offer Hanuman a place to land. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Mainaka rose, shining like the sun. Hanuman thought it was blocking his way and knocked against it — but then Mainaka spoke kindly, explaining that long ago Hanuman's father, the Wind, had saved it from danger, and now it wanted to repay that kindness with fruit and rest. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Hanuman was grateful, but he said he had promised not to stop until his task was done. He touched the mountain gently with his hand and flew on. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]

Next, a serpent-mother named Surasa blocked his way, telling him that she must eat anyone who passed. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] So Hanuman grew bigger to match her wide-open mouth — and she grew bigger too, again and again, until her mouth was a hundred yojanas wide. Then, quick as thought, Hanuman shrank down to the size of a thumb, darted inside her mouth, and popped right back out again before she could catch him! Surasa laughed with delight, for he had truly entered her mouth just as she asked, and she blessed him on his way. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]

Then a creature named Sinhika, who liked to catch travelers by their shadow, caught hold of Hanuman's shadow and stopped him in the air. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Hanuman looked down and understood who she was. He made himself huge to match her, and just as she opened her mouth wide to catch him, he made himself tiny and darted inside. He was far too strong for her, and she sank down into the sea, defeated. Hanuman, unharmed, flew on. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]

At last, Hanuman saw a shore ahead, covered with trees. He did not want the demons of Lanka to notice him, so before he landed he shrank back down to his ordinary size. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]] Standing on the shore at last, he looked up and saw Lanka shining on its mountain-top, as beautiful as Amaravati. [[ramayana:sundara:1:1]]
