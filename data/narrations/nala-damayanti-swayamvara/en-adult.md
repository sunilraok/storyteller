There was once a king of the Nishadhas named Nala, son of Virasena — strong, handsome, versed in the lore of horses, and possessed of every virtue a king could wish for; he stood at the head of all kings as the sun stands over the sky. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Far off among the Vidarbhas ruled another king, Bhima, a man of terrible prowess and great virtue, but he had no children, and for years he strove with all his heart to have issue. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] At last a Brahmarshi named Damana came to his court, and pleased by the reverence Bhima and his queen showed him, granted them a boon: a daughter who would be a jewel among women, and three splendid sons, Dama, Danta, and Damana. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] The daughter was named Damayanti, and as she grew she became famed through all the world for her beauty, her grace, and her good fortune, served by hundreds of maids as though she were the wife of Indra herself; nowhere among gods, Yakshas, or men had such beauty been seen or heard of before. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Nala too had no equal in the three worlds, for in beauty he was like the god of love in bodily form. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Travellers and heralds praised Nala before Damayanti and Damayanti before Nala, again and again, until each conceived a love for the other though they had never met, and that love only deepened with time. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]]

Unable to master the love growing in his heart, Nala took to wandering alone in the gardens of his palace, and there among a flock of golden-winged swans he caught one with his own hands. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] The swan begged, "Do not kill me, O king; I will do something to please you — I will speak of you before Damayanti so that she will never wish for another lord." Nala released it, and the swans rose up and flew to the city of the Vidarbhas. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] There they alighted before Damayanti and her maidens, who ran after them in delight; the swan Damayanti chased led her to a quiet place and spoke to her in a human voice, praising Nala's beauty and worth and telling her that the union of the best with the best would be a happy one. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Damayanti asked the swan to carry the same praise back to Nala, and the swan returned to the country of the Nishadhas and told Nala everything. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]]

From that day Damayanti lost her peace of mind. She sighed constantly, grew pale and thin, could not rest on her bed by day or night, and wept, saying only "Oh!" and "Alas!" [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]] Her handmaids, troubled by her state, hinted at the matter to King Bhima, who concluded that his daughter had come of age and resolved to hold her Swayamvara, her own choosing of a husband. [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]] He sent word to every king on earth, and they came to Vidarbha with their armies, their chariots, and their elephants, and Bhima received them all with honor. [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]]

At that same time, the celestial sages Narada and Parvata visited Indra in his heavenly hall, and told him of Damayanti's coming Swayamvara and of how every king in the world desired her hand. [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]] [[mahabharata:vana:54:2]] Hearing this, Indra and the other guardians of the world — Agni, Varuna, and Yama — resolved to go there themselves, and set out mounted on their chariots. [[mahabharata:vana:54:2]] Nala too, hearing of the great gathering, set out with a glad heart, his mind full of Damayanti. [[mahabharata:vana:54:2]] On the way the gods beheld him, radiant as the sun, and so struck were they by his beauty that they descended from the sky and asked him to become their messenger to Damayanti. [[mahabharata:vana:54:2]]

Nala gave his word, then asked who they were and what they wished of him. [[mahabharata:vana:55:1]] Indra answered that they were the gods themselves, come for Damayanti's sake — Indra, Agni, Varuna, and Yama — and asked Nala to tell her that the guardians of the world desired her, and that she should choose one of them for her lord. [[mahabharata:vana:55:1]] Nala protested that having come with the same wish himself, he could not bear to plead another's case before her, but the gods reminded him of his promise, and he could not refuse. [[mahabharata:vana:55:1]] [[mahabharata:vana:55:2]] Worried that her chambers were closely guarded, Nala asked how he could enter; Indra promised he would be able to, and by the gods' power he passed in unseen. [[mahabharata:vana:55:2]]

He found Damayanti among her maids, more radiant than the moon, and his own love grew at the sight of her, though he mastered his heart and kept his word. [[mahabharata:vana:55:2]] Her maidens, astonished by his splendor, could not bring themselves to speak to him, wondering aloud whether he were a god or a Gandharva. [[mahabharata:vana:55:2]] Damayanti, herself amazed, asked who he was and how he had passed her stern guards unseen, and Nala answered that his name was Nala, that he came as the messenger of the gods Indra, Agni, Varuna, and Yama, and that it was by their power alone he had entered; he asked her to choose one of them as her lord. [[mahabharata:vana:55:2]]

Damayanti, bowing to the gods, told Nala that she loved him and would give herself entirely to him, and that if he refused her, she would end her life by poison, fire, water, or the rope, for it was the swan's words about him that had set her heart burning. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]] Nala urged her instead to choose among the great gods, whose feet he was not fit to touch, warning that a mortal who displeases the gods comes to ruin, and praising each of the four in turn. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]] But Damayanti, weeping, bowed to all the gods and declared she chose Nala for her lord, truly and without doubt. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]] Bound by his given word to the gods, Nala could not accept her then, so Damayanti proposed a way that would leave no blame on him: that he come to the Swayamvara together with the gods, and there, before Indra and the Lokapalas, she would choose him openly. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]] Nala returned to the gods and told them all that had passed, and how Damayanti meant to choose him at the assembly in their very presence. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]]

On the appointed day, King Bhima summoned all the kings to the Swayamvara, and they entered the hall built with golden pillars, seating themselves in splendor. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] When Damayanti entered, dazzling every eye, and the kings' names were called, she saw five men seated together, alike in every feature, and could not tell which among them was Nala. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] In her distress she recalled what she had heard of the marks of the gods, but could see no such marks distinguishing any of the five before her, so she resolved to appeal to the gods themselves. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] With folded hands and a trembling voice she begged them, by the truth of her unwavering love for Nala since she first heard the swan's words, to reveal themselves and show her the true king of the Nishadhas. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Moved by the purity and fixed devotion of her heart, the gods granted her prayer and resumed their own signs: they stood without sweat, their eyes unwinking, their garlands unfading, their feet not touching the ground, while Nala alone was seen with his shadow beside him, his garland wilted, dust and sweat upon him, his feet resting on the earth, and his eyes blinking like any mortal's. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Knowing him thus, Damayanti went to Nala, caught the hem of his garment in shy joy, and placed a garland of flowers about his neck, choosing him for her husband before the whole assembly. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] The kings who had gathered cried out in disappointment, while the gods and great sages applauded, crying "Excellent! Excellent!" [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Nala, his heart full of gladness, comforted Damayanti, promising to be hers alone as long as life remained in him, and she paid him homage in words of the same devotion. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]]

Then the four gods, well pleased, each gave Nala two boons: Indra granted him the sight of his own godhead present at sacrifices and passage to the heavens thereafter; Agni granted his presence whenever Nala wished for it, and regions as bright as fire; Yama granted him a fine sense of taste in food and pre-eminence in virtue; and Varuna granted his presence at will and garlands of heavenly fragrance. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Having given these gifts, the gods departed to heaven, and the assembled kings, marvelling at Damayanti's choice, returned to their own lands. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Bhima, well pleased, then celebrated the wedding of Nala and Damayanti, and after some time Nala returned with Bhima's leave to his own city. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] There he ruled his people righteously and with great joy, performed the horse sacrifice and other rites with generous gifts to the Brahmanas, and in time Damayanti bore him a son and a daughter, both named Indrasena. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]]
