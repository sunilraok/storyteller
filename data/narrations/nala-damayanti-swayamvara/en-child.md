Long ago there lived a king named Nala. He ruled the Nishadha people, and he was strong, handsome, and kind. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Far away, in the land of Vidarbha, another king named Bhima had no children, though he wished for them very much. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] A wise sage named Damana visited him, and because Bhima and his queen honored the sage so well, the sage gave them a gift: a beautiful daughter named Damayanti, and three sons. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Damayanti grew up to be so lovely that people said no one so beautiful had ever been seen before, not even among the gods. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] Travelers told Nala how wonderful Damayanti was, and told Damayanti how wonderful Nala was, until the two of them fell in love without ever having met. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]]

One day, while Nala was walking alone in his garden, he saw swans with golden wings. He caught one gently in his hands. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] The swan begged, "Please let me go, and I will help you. I will tell Damayanti how wonderful you are." Nala let the swan go, and it flew away to Vidarbha with its flock. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]] There the swans landed near Damayanti, and when she chased after one, it spoke to her and praised Nala, saying that the two of them belonged together. Damayanti asked the swan to tell Nala the same, and it flew home and did. [[mahabharata:vana:53:1]]

After that, Damayanti could not stop thinking of Nala. She sighed all day, grew thin and pale, and cried often. [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]] Her maids noticed and told King Bhima, who understood that his daughter was ready to choose a husband. He announced a Swayamvara, a grand gathering where Damayanti would choose her own husband, and invited kings from everywhere. [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]]

Far above, in the heavens, the sage Narada told the god Indra about the Swayamvara. Indra and three other great gods — Agni, Varuna, and Yama — decided to go too, hoping to win Damayanti themselves. [[mahabharata:vana:54:1]] [[mahabharata:vana:54:2]] On the way, they saw Nala traveling to the Swayamvara, glowing with beauty, and they asked him to carry a message to Damayanti for them. [[mahabharata:vana:54:2]] Nala had already promised to help before he knew what they wanted, so even though it troubled him, he agreed. [[mahabharata:vana:55:1]] Using their power, the gods let him slip past the guards unseen, and he found Damayanti and gave her the gods' message: that she should choose one of them, Indra, Agni, Varuna, or Yama, as her husband. [[mahabharata:vana:55:2]]

But Damayanti told Nala that she loved only him, ever since she had heard the swan's words, and that she would choose no one else. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]] Nala, bound by his promise, told her to pick one of the great gods instead. Still, Damayanti had an idea: Nala should come to the Swayamvara together with the gods, and there, in front of everyone, she would choose him herself. That way no blame would fall on him. [[mahabharata:vana:56:1]]

On the day of the Swayamvara, many kings gathered in a beautiful hall. When Damayanti walked in, she saw five men who all looked exactly like Nala, for the four gods had taken his form. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] She did not know which one was the real Nala, so she prayed to the gods to show themselves truly, because her heart had never once wavered from Nala. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] The gods were moved by her love and showed their true signs: they did not sweat, their eyes never blinked, their garlands never wilted, and their feet never touched the ground. Only Nala stood on the earth like an ordinary man, with a tired garland, dusty feet, and blinking eyes. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Damayanti knew him at once. She walked to him and placed a garland of flowers around his neck, choosing him as her husband before everyone. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] The gods and sages cheered, "Excellent! Excellent!" and each of the four gods gave Nala a wonderful gift as a blessing. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]] Then Bhima happily held the wedding, and Nala took Damayanti home to be his queen, where they lived together in joy and later had a son and a daughter. [[mahabharata:vana:57:1]]
