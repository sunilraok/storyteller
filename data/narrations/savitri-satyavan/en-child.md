Princess Savitri set out on a long journey to find a husband, visiting many holy places, and came home to her father's palace, where the wise sage Narada was visiting. [[mahabharata:vana:292:1]] Her father asked her to say who she had chosen. [[mahabharata:vana:292:1]]

Savitri told them about a kind prince named Satyavan. His father, King Dyumatsena, had lost his eyesight and then lost his kingdom, so the family lived quietly in the forest. [[mahabharata:vana:292:2]] Satyavan had grown up there, and Savitri had chosen him in her heart. [[mahabharata:vana:292:2]]

But Narada said something sad: Satyavan was brave, wise, and good — but he had only one year left to live. [[mahabharata:vana:292:2]] Savitri's father begged her to choose someone else, but Savitri said a person can only choose a husband once, and she would not change her mind. [[mahabharata:vana:292:2]] Narada agreed that she should marry Satyavan, and then rose into the sky and went back to heaven. [[mahabharata:vana:292:2]][[mahabharata:vana:292:3]]

Savitri's father took her to the forest, and King Dyumatsena welcomed her as his son's bride. [[mahabharata:vana:293:1]] Savitri gave up her fine clothes for simple ones and lived happily with Satyavan's family, working hard and speaking kindly to everyone — but she never forgot what Narada had said. [[mahabharata:vana:293:1]]

As the day Narada spoke of came near, Savitri stopped eating for three days and nights, keeping a careful vow. [[mahabharata:vana:294:1]] When the day came, Satyavan picked up his axe to gather wood in the forest, and Savitri asked to go with him. His parents said yes. [[mahabharata:vana:294:1]][[mahabharata:vana:294:2]]

While Satyavan was cutting wood, his head began to hurt, and he lay down with his head on Savitri's lap to rest. [[mahabharata:vana:295:1]] Then Savitri saw someone frightening standing near them — a shining figure dressed in red, holding a rope. [[mahabharata:vana:295:1]] It was Yama, the god of death, and he told her that Satyavan's time had come. [[mahabharata:vana:295:1]][[mahabharata:vana:295:2]]

Yama gently pulled Satyavan's life out of his body and began walking away with it. Savitri got up and followed him, even though he told her to go back. [[mahabharata:vana:295:2]] She spoke to Yama with such wisdom and courage that he was pleased with her, and he offered her a gift — any gift except her husband's life. [[mahabharata:vana:295:2]]

Savitri asked for her father-in-law's eyesight back — he granted it. She kept following and asked for his kingdom back — granted. She asked for a hundred sons for her own father — granted. Then she asked for a hundred sons for herself, with Satyavan as their father — granted too! [[mahabharata:vana:295:2]] But then Savitri said this last gift could not come true without her husband alive, so she asked for one more thing: give me back Satyavan's life. [[mahabharata:vana:295:2]]

With a cheerful heart, Yama said, "So be it." He untied the rope and gave Satyavan back his life, promising the couple four hundred happy years together. [[mahabharata:vana:295:3]] Savitri hurried back to her husband, and he woke up as if from a long sleep. [[mahabharata:vana:295:3]]

Back at the hermitage, Dyumatsena had suddenly gotten his eyesight back and had been searching worriedly for his son all night. [[mahabharata:vana:296:1]] When Savitri and Satyavan finally arrived, everyone was overjoyed, and Savitri told them the whole wonderful story. [[mahabharata:vana:296:2]]

The next day, messengers came with more good news: the enemy who had taken Dyumatsena's kingdom had been defeated, and the people wanted their true king back. [[mahabharata:vana:297:1]] Dyumatsena returned to his throne, Satyavan ruled beside him, and in time Savitri had a hundred sons of her own. [[mahabharata:vana:297:1]] Because of her love and her clever words, Savitri saved her husband and brought good fortune to both families. [[mahabharata:vana:297:1]]
